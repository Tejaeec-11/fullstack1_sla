Where will IT be three years from now?

Everyone keeps asking whether IT is finished. I do not think it is finished. I think it is changing shape. The work that used to take ten people is starting to take three, and the three who stay are the ones who understand more than just the code in front of them. This page is my honest guess about what 2030 looks like from where I am standing in 2026.

What is actually changing

Three things are moving at the same time: the tools, the team size, and what a company is willing to pay for.

🤖

Tools write the easy code

Login pages, CRUD forms, basic dashboards — these are already half generated today. By 2030 a fresher who can only do this will have very little to sell.

🧩

Teams get smaller, roles get wider

Instead of separate frontend, backend, testing and deployment people, companies want one person who can move across all of it and still know what they are doing.

🔐

Judgement becomes the product

A machine can produce code. It cannot take responsibility when money is lost, data leaks, or a hospital system goes down. That responsibility is what a real engineer is paid for.

🛰️

IT stops being its own island

Software moves into energy, agriculture, defence, health and space. The person who knows one of those fields and can build software will be worth far more than someone who only knows frameworks.

How I think the road runs

Not a prediction from a report. Just what I can see happening around me.

2026

The tools get good enough to notice

Every student is already using AI to finish assignments. The gap between someone who understands their code and someone who pasted it starts to show in interviews.

2027

Fresher hiring gets stricter

Companies still hire, but they test differently. Less "write a for loop", more "here is broken code, find the mistake and explain why it broke".

2028

One developer does the work of three

Deployment, database, API and frontend all handled by the same person with AI assistance. Cloud knowledge stops being optional.

2029

The value moves to hard problems

Security, embedded systems, data pipelines, hardware-software integration. Things where a wrong answer costs real money or real lives, so a human has to sign off.

2030

IT becomes a layer under everything else

Space, power grids, electric vehicles, medical devices. Software is no longer the destination — it is the tool other industries use. Which is exactly why I am not planning to stop at web development.

My own opinion on this

People in my college keep saying IT is over and there is no point learning to code. I disagree, but not completely. What is over is the idea that you can learn one framework, get placed, and coast for ten years. That door is closing.

What is opening is different. If software is going to sit inside satellites, power systems and medical machines, then someone has to understand both sides — the hardware and the code. I studied Electrical and Electronics Engineering, and I am learning full-stack development on my own. Three years ago I would have thought those two things had nothing to do with each other. Now I think that combination is the point.

The question is not whether IT survives. It is whether you are still useful when the easy half of the job disappears.

So my plan is not to run away from IT. It is to stand where IT meets something physical — and for me that something is space technology. The next two pages explain what happens if I am wrong and IT collapses completely, and what I would do about it.
